## Install one

<Steps>
<Step>
### Choose New project → One-click apps

Pick an app and give it a name. The name becomes its address, like `blog.yourdomain.com`.
</Step>
<Step>
### Install, and wait for its first start

The first install downloads the image, so it can take a few minutes. The log shows how far along it is.
</Step>
<Step>
### Do its first-run step straight away

The project page shows what to do until you mark it done.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout>

Don't skip the first-run step. These apps are on the public internet from the moment they start, and on most of them the first visitor becomes the admin. Umami starts with a well-known password, and Vaultwarden lets anyone sign up until you turn that off.

</Callout>

## After installing

- **It's a normal project.** Logs, metrics, domains, limits and rollback all work, and its settings are yours to change.
- **Files and databases stay across deploys.** App data sits on a [persistent volume](/volumes) or in a managed database. Managed databases and volumes both get [daily backups](/backups).
- **Upgrades are up to you.** The version is pinned, so an app only changes when you change its image under **Settings** and redeploy. Check the app's release notes first.
- **Adding a custom domain?** Several apps store their address in an environment variable: Ghost (`url`), n8n (`WEBHOOK_URL`), Vaultwarden (`DOMAIN`), Gitea (`GITEA__server__ROOT_URL`), NocoDB (`NC_SITE_URL`), Open WebUI (`WEBUI_URL`), Linkwarden (`NEXTAUTH_URL`), WordPress (`WORDPRESS_CONFIG_EXTRA`), Mattermost (`MM_SERVICESETTINGS_SITEURL`), Metabase (`MB_SITE_URL`), Grafana (`GF_SERVER_ROOT_URL`), Directus (`PUBLIC_URL`) and Miniflux (`BASE_URL`). Update it under **Environment** and redeploy.
- **Deleting the project keeps its database.** Delete that separately under **Databases** when you're sure you don't need the data.
